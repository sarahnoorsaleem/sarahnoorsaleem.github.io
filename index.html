Current Research

As a Research Assistant at the University of Toronto Mississauga, I collaborate with a multidisciplinary research team investigating perceived and objective walkability among Arab adults in the Greater Toronto Area.

My work includes participant recruitment and engagement, community outreach across the eastern and western Greater Toronto Area, participatory mapping, map digitization in ArcGIS Pro, survey-data preparation, transcription, and mixed-methods research support.

I have digitized more than 30 participant-generated maps to prepare spatial datasets for analysis and contribute to ongoing collaborative research manuscripts.

Graduate Research Direction

My proposed graduate research examines how Pakistani immigrants recreate home and belonging in Mississauga and how their cultural destinations and activity spaces compare with those they experienced in Pakistan.

I am interested in using participatory GIS and qualitative interviews to identify culturally meaningful destinations associated with religion, food, community, recreation, and everyday routines. These locations could be used to reconstruct approximate pre-migration and post-migration activity spaces and examine how everyday places contribute to belonging, cultural continuity, and the recreation of ghar, or home, across places.

This research direction builds on my undergraduate work examining perceived walkability, cultural destinations, and neighbourhood experiences among racialized communities in the Greater Toronto Area.
