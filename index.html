Current Research

As a Research Assistant at the University of Toronto Mississauga, I collaborate with a multidisciplinary research team investigating perceived and objective walkability among Arab adults in the Greater Toronto Area. My work includes participant recruitment and engagement, community outreach across the eastern and western GTA, participatory mapping, map digitization in ArcGIS Pro, survey-data preparation, transcription, and mixed-methods research support.

I have digitized more than 30 participant-generated maps to prepare spatial datasets for analysis and support ongoing collaborative manuscripts.

Proposed Graduate Research

My proposed graduate research examines how Pakistani immigrants recreate home and belonging in Mississauga and how their cultural destinations and activity spaces compare with those they experienced in Pakistan.

Using participatory GIS and qualitative interviews, I am interested in mapping culturally meaningful destinations associated with religion, food, community, recreation, and everyday routines. The project would compare approximate pre-migration and post-migration activity spaces while examining how everyday places contribute to belonging, cultural continuity, and the recreation of ghar, or home, across places.
